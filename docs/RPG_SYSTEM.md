# SAO RPG system — design direction

Scope: singleplayer-first living MMORPG in Minecraft 1.21.1, NeoForge. Player and autonomous NPC progression must share authoritative rules. This is a design specification, not a claim that the systems already exist. Current class-based combat, practice, needs and simulated economy are prototypes; migrate them incrementally and preserve existing saves and identities.

## Agreed direction

- Character levels have no fixed gameplay cap. Increasing XP costs and diminishing attribute returns keep combat, movement, reach and tick budgets bounded. Use overflow-safe progression and explicitly handle numeric storage limits; do not silently wrap or erase progression.
- Character XP is separate from vanilla Minecraft XP. Earn it through meaningful combat, quests, discoveries, crafting and professions, not just fighting. Noncombat specialists can become high-level characters. Actual combat mastery remains separate.
- No permanent combat classes: equipment determines the active weapon family and fighting style. NPCs have preferences, not immutable restrictions. Switching equipment retains character progression and previously learned masteries; it never grants skills from an unfamiliar weapon.
- Independent weapon family/style and profession proficiency, skill trees and loadouts. NPCs choose attributes and skills using goals, personality, equipment and experience. Players can choose their own builds. Advanced skills require the correct equipment, proficiency and prerequisites. No all-family unlock from character level.
- Believable fantasy weapon compatibility: explicit one-handed, two-handed or versatile grip; weight, reach, handling, stamina and attribute/proficiency requirements. Strong characters cannot override two-handed restrictions. Supported dual wielding includes two one-handed swords, compatible sword/dagger, paired daggers, light axes and small sickles; prohibit paired greatswords, full scythes and other two-handed weapons. Shield compatibility depends on grip. Dual wielding has independent mastery, separate hand strikes and durability, not automatic double damage. Full-sized scythes are two-handed. Unusual pairings require authored compatibility and animation support.
- Broad extensible catalog covering swords (straight, curved, katana, rapier, sabre, greatsword), daggers, axes, maces, clubs, hammers, flails, spears, pikes, polearms, tridents, scythes, sickles, bows, crossbows, thrown weapons, unarmed, gauntlets, claws, shields and optional exotic/magic families. Add families via data-driven definitions and validated adapters; do not claim every weapon is implemented or allow unknown mod items to bypass grip rules.
- Profession mastery for crafting/blacksmithing, gathering, cooking, brewing, healing, commerce, fishing and other meaningful professions. Keep service NPCs focused on supporting MMORPG gameplay rather than importing Colony Overhaul's construction/production simulator.
- Monster levels and difficulty depend on floor, habitat, species and rank (common, elite, named, mini-boss, dungeon boss, guardian), not the current player level. Weak enemies remain weak; rewards taper with level gap. NPCs may surpass the player while the world is ticking. Preserve sequential floor unlock and actual guardian victory requirements until a verified party-clear system replaces them.
- Non-hardcore defeat: no permanent player or NPC death, no lost character levels, mastery, inventory or Col. Downed revival, interrupted expedition, recovery and modest equipment wear. A safe-settlement/inn return for unrecovered characters, with no duplication of inventories. Proposed defaults: 60 seconds downed, revival at 30% health, 5% condition loss on failed revival/defeat; balance and accessibility to be tested. Ordinary Minecraft death handling outside the intended Aincrad scope must not be silently changed.
- Inspectable NPC profile: level/XP, attributes, weapon and profession masteries, skills, gear, needs, quest, party, guild and relationship information. Player UI should show equivalent own-character data. Avoid exposing fictional stats as verified before implementation.

## Combat design (approved direction; numerical values not finalized)

### One shared combat resolver
Separate attack intent, windup, active hit window, recovery and cooldown. Server-authoritative validation for equipment, grip, reach, line of sight, stamina, target and safe zone. Rendered animations and sound follow the authoritative result; never deal duplicate damage because an animation callback fires twice. Players use input actions; NPCs use the same action interface and constraints. NPC AI has reaction delay, imperfect aim, limited knowledge and ability selection based on personality, training and actual threats. Off-screen simulation cannot silently manufacture observed monster kills, guardian wins or item loot; design a separate resource-accounted abstract encounter policy before enabling remote combat.

### Stamina, mana and defense
Stamina governs sprinting, attacks, dodging, blocking and advanced weapon skills. Light ordinary attacks have manageable costs, heavy actions cost more. Regenerate during genuine recovery, not infinite spam. Below required stamina, fail or downgrade the action according to a documented rule; no negative stamina. Heavy weapons trade recovery time for reach/impact. Shield blocks are directional, consume stamina and permit guard breaks; weapon parries depend on appropriate equipped weapon and timing. A successful parry creates a short counter window, not a guaranteed free kill. Directional dodge has short, bounded protection, cannot chain permanent immunity or pass through walls, and should be accessible with configurable timing. Magic, if implemented, uses a separate mana resource and cast/recovery rules. No player speed or attack speed grows unbounded with level.

### Skills and weapon mastery
Basic attacks available with compatible weapons; specialized moves unlock from that family/style's practice and skill prerequisites. Skills equip into a limited active loadout and have costs, cooldowns, animation and targeting rules. Switching weapons changes active loadout without resetting cooldowns or creating an extra attack. Cross-family fundamentals may share limited basic proficiency, never unlock advanced nodes. Dual blades use alternating hands, combination attacks and separate durability; advanced SAO-inspired multi-hit skills require actual mastery, reasonable stamina costs and a bounded sequence of real hit windows. Do not promise exact anime animations or special abilities until authored and tested.

### Character build balance
Separate base character attributes (proposed strength, vitality, agility, dexterity, intelligence, endurance, luck), equipment statistics, mastery, and temporary buffs. Allow profession-focused attributes and skills to remain useful outside combat; high character level alone must not confer complete weapon competence. Curves, point allocation, damage formulas, XP amounts, monster ranges and equipment thresholds require a shared balance test. Preserve leveling while preventing runaway movement, health, hit rate and combat speed.

## Additional systems approved

- Item progression: common through legendary rarity, item quality, material tiers, affixes, durability, crafting mastery and fair trade/repair. Avoid random loot invalidating crafting and avoid equipment-level restrictions that contradict attribute/proficiency requirements.
- Parties: front-line defense, damage, ranged/support and healing roles derived from equipped skills rather than permanent classes. Shared encounter credit based on contribution, including support; no duplicate rewards or last-hit-only XP. NPC recruitment, leadership, morale, recovery, retreat and resource distribution. Guilds and NPC floor progression use actual accepted achievements.
- Monster behavior: readable attack telegraphs, different attacks, habitat and level bands, bounded spawn density. Elites and bosses gain mechanics rather than just HP. Boss difficulty is a deliberate authored challenge, not universal level scaling.
- Economy: actual earned quest rewards, stock sources and real equipment/items; no free minting by repeated crafting, selling and rebuying or reload loops. Design renewable supply sources before claiming endless economy.
- Reputation and discovery: first-visit IDs, meaningful quest completion, social consequences and merchant/guild reputation. Recipe/material/quality-aware XP; trivial spam and repeated rediscovery must not grant infinite cheap XP. For crafting, award only on actual completed output, not recipe preview or fake production.
- UI and controls: accessible status bars and readable level/rank/health display, inspectable NPC panels, configurable controls for dodge/block/parry and skill slots. Decide whether to use native animations, optional integrations, or both before authoring all weapon moves.

## Incremental implementation and acceptance

1. Stabilize current melee/archery and first custom monster; retain existing geometry/identity and test evidence.
2. Add save-backed character XP, level derivation, attribute allocation and independent mastery with migration from existing practice. Keep historical prototype practice distinct from earned combat XP; no retroactive fabricated kills. Pure tests for monotonic curves, numeric boundaries, deduplication, reload and equivalent player/NPC rules.
3. Add real XP event sources for combat, quests, discovery and verified completed crafting/profession work; test duplicate and trivial-action rejection. Initially distinguish fully implemented sources from future adapters. Include support contributions only when party attribution exists.
4. Implement shared equipment/grip/compatibility resolver and weapon families. Replace permanent class restrictions with preference and actual loadout. Preserve working combat until new state and runtime behavior are accepted.
5. Introduce stamina and combat action state machine, then blocking/parry/dodge, actual skill activation and authored animations. Validate server/client agreement, melee and ranged, safe zones, incorrect targets, stamina, wrong weapon, switch/cooldown, death/retreat and damage attribution.
6. Add skill trees and active loadouts for a small representative set first (sword, shield, dual swords, bow, scythe), then extend by validated family. Include a separate profession tree example and inspectable character data. Real UI, input, animation and visual tests.
7. Add downed/revival/recovery for players and NPCs after core combat works; test save/load, safe respawn, no inventory duplication, non-hardcore behavior and no progression loss.
8. Add fixed monster levels, difficulty profiles, loot quality, party XP distribution and renewable economy/quest sources with balance and performance tests. Expand custom monster models and floor content in verified slices.

Never mark a feature playable from a design document or unit test. Run Minecraft GameTests, dedicated-server restart tests and actual client probes where applicable. Keep screenshots and visual approval separate. Prefer bounded simulation, no forced distant chunk loads, and meaningful autonomous progress while the world is running.

## Solo play — approved requirement

Solo adventurers (player and NPC) are first-class. Do not require joining a party, inviting NPCs, or joining a guild for ordinary quests, travel, leveling, equipment, professions or access to any floor boss. Fixed monster difficulty: no automatic level/HP weakening just because someone plays alone. Bosses must have a mechanically achievable solo path with telegraphed attacks, dodge/block/counter opportunities and recovery consumables; difficult boss balance remains to be tested, not a claim that all bosses exist. Party cooperation offers tactical advantages and contribution rewards; solo keeps own earned loot and credit. Support independent NPC lifestyles, temporary cooperation, persistent parties and guilds with personality-driven preferences. Defeat without a rescuer uses the non-hardcore settlement recovery path, no mandatory healer. Run solo and party acceptance tests separately. No new party or boss implementation yet.

## Approved material and weapon direction — v0.2.36 candidate

Native equipment is the foundation; optional external weapons use explicit family adapters, original movesets and server combat rules. Separate material properties, rarity, craftsmanship, shape/grip, mastery and moveset. Rare named unique weapons have a persistent single-world identity in the future, exceptional crafted commissions as well as boss drops, exploration and events; their special stance/moveset requires mastery and familiarity. Planned separate families include tachi, uchigatana, odachi, crescent blades (light, heavy, polearm, ringblade, paired), swordstaff, double-bladed weapons and exotic families. Defensive abilities are anatomy/equipment/skill-specific, not universal monster blocking. No family or unique behavior is claimed implemented by this entry.

Material bands (proposed balance; not world generation): copper 1–5, tin 1–8, iron 1–10, gold 1–15, silver 3–15, mithril 10–25, adamantite 20–40, orichalcum 35–55, celestium 50–75, astralite 75–100. Lower-tier materials remain useful for alloys, upgrades, trade and special commissions. Steel uses actual vanilla iron and carbon (coal or charcoal) in a shapeless recipe for a single unrefined steel bloom, refined with separate fuel and time in a blast furnace for one steel ingot. No extra yield, no free items, no new ore blocks or generation yet. The first native blade will use this real steel. MaterialRules defines provisional properties relative to iron (edge, mass, durability, forging difficulty); quality modifies edge/durability independently of rarity. Material attributes are metadata, not player damage modifiers. Native items have original icons and descriptive tooltips. Mining/metallurgy XP, ore worldgen bands, tin/bronze and custom forge/NPC resource-accounted production remain future. Existing XP and NPC state unchanged.

## v0.2.47 candidate — skill selection and durable combat

Four single-target one-handed sword skills now share the native action resolver: Horizontal (mastery 3, 26 stamina, 160% damage, 100 ticks); Slant (4, 20, 140%, 90); Vertical (5, 32, 185%, 120); Sonic Leap (7, 34, 155%, 160). Values are provisional; all require compatible equipment, valid target and one real hit window. Each skill has its own cooldown and a 30-tick shared skill delay; ordinary attack recovery still applies. B cycles one active slot through actually learned skills while idle, R activates the selection, and the SAO HUD displays its mastery requirement or actual cooldown. Both bindings are reconfigurable. NPC sword users can choose unlocked moves from the same library. This is a single active slot with mastery gates, not a complete branching skill tree or multi-slot loadout UI. No automatic monster blocking or new monster defense.

Sonic Leap uses a small ground-level native velocity impulse, not teleportation: up to 4.1-block initial target range, six collision/support/wilderness samples along the intended corridor, and a normal 2.8-block final hit range with actual forward horizontal displacement. Player skill targeting uses the server-owned aim ray and blocks/intervening living entities; no client-provided hit position or damage. Distinct third-person pose curves have been authored for the three new actions; first person retains native rendering.

A new sparse SwordCombatData SavedData record keys stamina, recovery, selected skill and all cooldowns by durable player/NPC identity. Reload or re-projection cancels unfinished attacks, counters and combos, not their costs or cooldowns. Bounded elapsed world ticks permit rest; paused wall-clock time does not. Existing saves without the new file start with the default combat state. No cross-file crash-atomic guarantee. A Minecraft GameTest covers identity separation, saved state round-trip, duplicate rejection and sparse default removal.

## v0.2.48 candidate — confirmed four-strike sword skills

Horizontal Square (sword mastery 9, 52 stamina, 220-tick cooldown) and Vertical Square (mastery 11, 60 stamina, 260-tick cooldown) join the four existing single-target skills. Horizontal Square schedules impacts at 8/22/36/50 ticks with damage multipliers .85/.85/.85/1.05 and completes recovery at tick 68; Vertical Square schedules 10/24/38/52 with .95/.95/.95/1.20 and completes recovery at tick 72. The deliberately conservative 14-tick spacing allows native sword recharge and ordinary Minecraft hit immunity, rather than resetting target immunity or manufacturing simultaneous hits. Each real strike requires the same live target, compatible held weapon, facing, range, line of sight and safe-zone validation. The next strike is armed only by confirmation of actual health/absorption loss. Missed, stale, interrupted or invalid hit windows cancel the remaining sequence; actual death or a broken sword stops follow-ups. No stamina refund, cooldown reset or ordinary combo credit. Both players and represented NPCs use the same resolver; the full cost is charged once. Native item wear and damage attribution remain intact. The current one-slot B/R selection is unchanged; new skills enter the selection only at the requisite mastery. HUD reports actual confirmed strikes out of four; original synchronized pose curves and bounded native sweep/enchantment particles accompany real strikes, not separate damage triggers. Third-person arm poses remain prototypes, not a full body/first-person animation library.

Combat save schema 2 migrates exactly four legacy cooldowns from schema 1 by retaining their enum order and appending two zero timers. New checkpoints store all six skill cooldowns, selection, stamina and recovery; no unfinished attack survives reload.
